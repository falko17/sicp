\begin{displayquote}
	Show that under the assumption of small percentage tolerances there is a simple formula for the approximate percentage tolerance of the product of two intervals in terms of the tolerances of the factors. You may simplify the problem by assuming that all numbers are positive.
\end{displayquote}

So, we have two intervals, $i_1 = (a_1, b_1)$ and $i_2 = (a_2, b_2)$, with $a_1, a_2, b_1, b_2 \in \mathbb{N}$.
We can also express these as $c \pm p$, where $p$ is the uncertainty in percent.
Essentially, $i = c \pm (p \cdot c)$.
We hence also know $p = \frac{\frac{b - a}{2}}{c} = \frac{b-a}{2 \cdot c}$.

According to the exercise description, we can apparently find a formula for the approximate tolerance of the product of two intervals using only the ,,input'' tolerances.
Let's see if we can get there.

Since all numbers are positive, we know (see 2.11) that $i_{\times} = i_1 \cdot i_2 = (a_1 \cdot a_2, b_1 \cdot b_2)$.
Hence:
% (99.0, 101) => (100, 0.01)
% (190, 210) => (200, 0.05)
% => (18810, 21210) => (20010, 0.12)

% (0.9, 1.1) => (1.0, 0.1)
% (89.1, 111.1) => (100.1, 0.11)  [from 0.01 & 0.10]

% Or: (171, 231) => (201, 0.15)  [from 0.05 & 0.10]

\begin{align*}
	i_{\times} & = (a_1 \cdot a_2, b_1 \cdot b_2)                                                                         \\
	           & = ((c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2), (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)) \\
	           & = ((c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2), (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)) \\
\end{align*}

Reformulating this into ,,center-percent'' notation:
\begin{align*}
	c_\times & = \frac{(c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2) + (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)}{2}                                                                                                                       \\
	p_\times & = \frac{(c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2) - (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)}{c_\times \cdot 2}                                                                                                        \\
	         & = \frac{(c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2) - (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)}{\frac{(c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2) + (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)}{2} \cdot 2} \\
	         & = \frac{(c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2) - (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)}{(c_1 - p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 - p_2 \cdot c_2) + (c_1 + p_1 \cdot c_1) \cdot (c_2 + p_2 \cdot c_2)}
\end{align*}
